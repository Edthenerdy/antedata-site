Published evidence — what projects actually cost

Cited research

Baselines differ by source (first-announced cost vs approved budget) and several figures are from secondary summaries of paywalled primaries — treat as directional. The structural finding: the median IT overrun is ≈0%, but the mean is +27% to +80% because the tail follows a power law (α ≈ 2.1–2.3) — roughly one project in six becomes a "black swan" averaging +200%. Single-point contingency cannot price that tail; simulating it against a reference class is the point of this product.
How this becomes a product: the engine runs in the browser; the moat is the calibration loop over data the client already owns. An AI layer (Claude API) reads the register, matches the project against the client's own delivery history inside their tenancy, falls back to anonymised industry aggregates where history is thin, and challenges optimistic entries before the board sees P80. Correlation between risks ships in this prototype (two-level Gaussian copula — systemic driver across all risks, stronger within categories; Iman–Conover reordering, so marginals are preserved and tails widen honestly). Production roadmap versus incumbents: distribution fitting from actuals, schedule risk (P6 / MS Project), multi-output WBS models, and audit-ready reporting.
